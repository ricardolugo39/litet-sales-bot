What Helium adds to the October 17 review

These are fixed research findings, not a live Helium feed. Their dates and scope differ from the financial baseline above; they do not automatically update when sales data changes. The lines in the map identify questions, not proven causes.

Search demand was falling before the change

Estimated trailing 30-day Amazon searches, reported Oct 10 → Oct 17:

Cycling socks: 10,391 → 6,090 (−41%)
Cycling socks for men: 3,385 → 1,934 (−43%)
White cycling socks: 867 → 555 (−36%)

These overlapping estimates are market context, not our sessions or searches lost in one week. The latest available point was Oct 17, so it does not measure post-change demand.

Organic visibility held up

Average observed organic rank for the tracked LITET parent, Oct 1–16 → Oct 18–26:

Cycling socks: 26.3 → 18.4
White cycling socks: 6.6 → 4.9

Lower is better. Snapshots do not measure impressions or visits. The white three-pack child ASIN was not individually tracked; parent rank cannot establish that variation's rank.

Investigate how ad spend is used

White cycling socks: 16 clicks, 6 attributed orders, $33.61 spend and $217.94 attributed sales (15.4% ACoS).

Road cycling socks men, broad: 41 clicks, 3 attributed orders, $79.29 spend and $86.97 attributed sales (91.2% ACoS).

Oct 1–19 search-term data, reviewed Oct 27. Small samples; attributed purchases may include other products. Confirm the current target, bid and advertised product before any changes. ACoS is ad spend ÷ ad-attributed sales; TACOS uses total sales.

Takeaway: Lower estimated demand may have contributed to lower traffic, while the tracked parent's organic rank improved on these terms. Paid clicks cost more, and traffic and conversion also need attention. Consider testing relevant traffic within the existing budget, then judge the result by contribution after ads, three-pack sales and TACOS.
